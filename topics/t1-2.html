Exponents, logarithms and the binomial theorem

Guiding questionHow do logarithms invert exponential growth, and how can we extract a single power of x from a high-degree binomial without expanding the entire bracket?

What you need to be able to do

Tick each skill when you can do it without looking at your notes. All skills in Topic 1.2 are SL core and frequently tested on Paper 1 (without a calculator).

  • Manipulate expressions with integer, negative, and fractional exponents (a^{m/n}).
  • Convert fluently between exponential form a^x = b and logarithmic form x = log_a b.
  • Evaluate logarithms numerically with a GDC on Paper 2, for example log_7 50 or ln 0.3.
  • Simplify expressions and solve equations using the product, quotient, power, and change-of-base laws of logarithms.
  • Spot and solve disguised quadratic equations in a^x or log_a x, checking domain restrictions (a^x > 0, log argument > 0).
  • Expand (a + b)^n for n ∈ ℤ+ using Pascal’s triangle or nCr, and find any target term or constant term using the general term T_{r+1}.

“Solve 9^x - 10(3^x) + 9 = 0” or “Solve e^{2x} - 5e^x + 6 = 0” Disguised quadratic in u = 3^x or u = e^x since 9^x = (3^2)^x = (3^x)^2. Let u = a^x (u > 0), factor the quadratic in u, reject any u <= 0, then solve a^x = u.
“Solve log_2 x + 6 log_x 2 = 5” or logs with different bases log_2 x, log_8 x Change of base to a single common base (usually the smallest base). Replace log_x 2 = 1 / log_2 x and log_8 x = log_2 x / log_2 8 = (1/3) log_2 x.
“Express ln(a^2 / sqrt(b)) in terms of p = ln a and q = ln b” Split products into sums, quotients into differences, and bring powers to the front. ln(a^2 b^{-1/2}) = 2 ln a - (1/2) ln b = 2p - (1/2)q.
“Find the constant term / term independent of x in (ax^p + b/x^q)^n” The term in the expansion where the net exponent of x equals 0. Write T_{r+1} = nCr (ax^p)^{n-r}(bx^{-q})^r, set the exponent p(n-r) - qr = 0 to find r, then evaluate.
“Find the coefficient of x^k in (c + dx)(a + bx)^n” Product of two brackets: pair c with the x^k term and dx with the x^{k-1} term. Compute c × [coeff of x^k] + d × [coeff of x^{k-1}]. Watch signs carefully!
SL 1.5 & 1.7

Laws of exponents, logarithms and change of base

For any positive base a > 0 (a != 1), a logarithm answers the question: “To what power must I raise a to get b?”

Before you start: disguised quadratics such as e^{2x} - 5e^x + 6 = 0 need factorising, the quadratic formula and the discriminant from 2.2 Quadratics, rational, exponential and log models, where the graphs of y = a^x and y = log_a x are also studied.

Sections 1.5 and 1.7 of your Formula Booklet give the equivalence between exponents and logarithms, the three core log laws, and the change-of-base formula:

ax = b ⇔ x = loga b (a > 0, b > 0, a ≠ 1)
loga(xy) = loga x + loga y, loga(x / y) = loga x − loga y, loga(xm) = m loga x
loga x = logb xlogb a (change of base)

Two special bases have their own shorthand: log x = log_{10} x (common logarithm) and ln x = log_e x (natural logarithm, where Euler’s constant e ≈ 2.718). Also memorize the instant identities log_a(1) = 0, log_a(a) = 1 and log_b(a) = 1 / log_a(b). You do not need to memorize a^{log_a x} = x: it is printed in Section 2.9 of the Formula Booklet.

Integer exponents (SL 1.5). These laws are not in the Formula Booklet: a^m × a^n = a^{m+n}, a^m / a^n = a^{m-n}, (a^m)^n = a^{mn}, a^0 = 1 and a^{-n} = 1 / a^n. On Paper 2 you must also be able to evaluate logarithms numerically with your GDC, for example log_7 50 = ln 50 / ln 7 ≈ 2.01 and ln 0.3 ≈ -1.20.

Beware of fake logarithm laws and missing domain checks:
1. log_a(x + y) does not simplify! Never write log_a(x + y) = log_a x + log_a y.
2. (ln x)^2 is (ln x) × (ln x), whereas ln(x^2) = 2 ln x.
3. Whenever you combine log_a(f(x)) + log_a(g(x)) into log_a(f(x)g(x)), you must test your final roots in the original equation and reject any value of x that makes f(x) <= 0 or g(x) <= 0.

Solving a logarithmic equation with different bases

Solve the equation log_2(x) + log_x(8) = 4 for x. [5 marks]

  1. Both logarithms must share the same base before we can solve algebraically.

    logx(8) = log2(8)log2(x) = 3log2(x) [M1 A1]

  2. Multiplying through by u clears the fraction and gives a factorable quadratic.

    u + 3u = 4 ⇒ u2 − 4u + 3 = 0 ⇒ (u − 1)(u − 3) = 0 [M1 A1]

  3. Use log₂(x) = u ⇔ x = 2^u for each root.

    log2(x) = 1 ⇒ x = 2, or log2(x) = 3 ⇒ x = 23 = 8 [A1]

x = 2 or x = 8

Solve log_2(x) + 4 log_x(2) = 5 and enter the larger of the two solutions for x.

  1. Rewrite log_x(2) = 1 / log_2(x) and let u = log_2(x).
  2. Solve u^2 - 5u + 4 = 0 to get u = 1 or u = 4, then compute x = 2^u.
u + 4/u = 5 ⇒ (u − 1)(u − 4) = 0 ⇒ u = 1 or u = 4
x = 21 = 2 or x = 24 = 16
SL 1.9

The binomial theorem for positive integers

When a positive integer power (a + b)^n is expanded, there are n + 1 terms. As you move from left to right across the expansion, the power of a steps down from n to 0, the power of b steps up from 0 to n (so the two exponents always add up to n), and the coefficients come from row n of Pascal’s triangle (counting the top 1 as row 0) or the combination formula nCr.

Section 1.9 of your Formula Booklet gives the binomial expansion and the combination formula:

(a + b)n = an + nC1 an−1b + … + nCr an−rbr + … + bn
nCr = n!r!(n − r)!
n
positive integer power (n ∈ ℤ+)
r
term index running from r = 0 to r = n
Tr+1
general (r + 1)-th term of the expansion
Tr+1 = nCr an−r br (general term: not in the booklet, so learn it)

How to isolate any target term without expanding the whole bracket:
1. Write down the general term T_{r+1} = nCr (a)^{n-r} (b)^r, enclosing the entire second term (including its minus sign and fractional power of x) inside (b)^r.
2. Separate the numerical factors from the powers of x.
3. Set the combined exponent of x equal to the target power (or 0 for the constant term), solve for r, and substitute r back in.

Finding the constant term in a binomial with a reciprocal term

Find the constant term in the expansion of (3x - 2/x^2)^6. [5 marks]

  1. Rewrite −2/x² as −2x⁻² so all powers of x can be combined using exponent laws.

    Tr+1 = 6Cr (3x)6−r (−2x−2)r = 6Cr (3)6−r (−2)r x6−r−2r [M1 A1]

  2. A constant term is independent of x, meaning its exponent on x is 0.

    6 − 3r = 0 ⇒ r = 2 [M1 A1]

  3. Compute ⁶C₂, 3⁴, and (−2)² carefully.

    6C2 × 34 × (−2)2 = 15 × 81 × 4 = 4860 [A1]

Find the constant term in the expansion of (3x - 1/x^2)^6.

  1. As in the worked example above, the power of x is x^{6-3r}, so the constant term occurs at r = 2.
  2. Evaluate ^6C_2 × (3)^{6-2} × (-1)^2.
r = 2: 6C2 × 34 × (−1)2 = 15 × 81 × 1 = 1215
