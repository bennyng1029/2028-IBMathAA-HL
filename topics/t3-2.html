The unit circle, trig identities and periodic models

Guiding questionHow does rotating a point around a unit circle turn right-angled triangle ratios into periodic waves that model tides, seasons, and oscillations?

What you need to be able to do

Tick each skill when you can do it without looking at your notes. Items marked AHL are tested on Higher Level papers only.

  • Define cos theta, sin theta, and tan theta on the unit circle and state exact values for multiples of 0, pi/6, pi/4, pi/3, pi/2 across all four quadrants.
  • Apply tan theta = sin theta / cos theta, cos^2 theta + sin^2 theta = 1, and the double-angle identities for sin 2theta and cos 2theta.
  • Sketch and build periodic models y = a sin(b(x - c)) + d and y = a cos(b(x - c)) + d from amplitude, period, phase shift, and principal axis.
  • Solve linear, quadratic, and multiple-angle trigonometric equations analytically and graphically on a finite interval.
  • Use reciprocal ratios sec theta, cosec theta, cot theta and Pythagorean identities 1 + tan^2 theta = sec^2 theta and 1 + cot^2 theta = cosec^2 theta.
  • Expand and simplify expressions using compound-angle identities sin(A +- B), cos(A +- B), tan(A +- B) and tan 2theta.
  • State the restricted domains and principal ranges of arcsin x, arccos x, arctan x, sketch their graphs, and simplify compositions like cos(arcsin x).

SL 3.5–3.6

The unit circle, exact ratios and fundamental identities

Before you start: you need radian measure, arc length and the exact ratios from right-angled triangles in 3.1 3D geometry, triangle trigonometry and radians.

The unit circle has centre (0, 0) and radius 1. If a ray from the origin makes an angle theta counter-clockwise from the positive x-axis and meets the unit circle at P(x, y), then by definition:

x = cos θ,    y = sin θ,    tan θ = sin θcos θ = yx   (gradient of OP)
cos²θ + sin²θ = 1
Pythagoras' theorem x^2 + y^2 = 1 on the unit circle
sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ
Double-angle identity for sine
cos 2θ
cos^2 theta - sin^2 theta = 2 cos^2 theta - 1 = 1 - 2 sin^2 theta

Exact trigonometric values (Paper 1 must-know table)

You are not given exact values in the Formula Booklet. An easy memory trick for the first quadrant is the square-root-over-2 ladder: for theta = 0, pi/6, pi/4, pi/3, pi/2, sin theta is sqrt(0)/2, sqrt(1)/2, sqrt(2)/2, sqrt(3)/2, sqrt(4)/2, and cos theta counts backwards!

Using quadrant signs and double-angle identities without finding θ

Given that sin theta = -3/5 and pi < theta < 3pi/2 (Quadrant III), find the exact values of:
(a) cos theta [2 marks]    (b) sin 2theta [2 marks]    (c) cos 2theta [2 marks]

  1. In Quadrant III (π < θ < 3π/2), both x = cos θ and y = sin θ are negative.

    cos2θ = 1 − (−35)2 = 1 − 925 = 1625 ⇒ cos θ = −45 [M1 A1]

  2. Since both sin θ and cos θ are negative, their product is positive (2θ lies in Quadrant I/II).

    sin 2θ = 2(−35)(−45) = 2425 [M1 A1]

  3. Using 1 − 2 sin²θ uses the given value of sin θ directly and avoids carrying any slip from part (a).

    cos 2θ = 1 − 2(−35)2 = 1 − 1825 = 725 [M1 A1]

(a) −4/5; (b) 24/25; (c) 7/25

[Paper 1 — No GDC] Evaluate the exact value of cos(4*pi/3).

  1. 4*pi/3 = pi + pi/3 lies in Quadrant III, with acute reference angle alpha = pi/3 (60°).
  2. In Quadrant III, cosine is negative, so cos(4*pi/3) = -cos(pi/3).
cos(4π3) = −cos(π3) = −12 = −0.5
SL 3.7–3.8

Sinusoidal periodic models and solving trigonometric equations

Before you start: the parameters a, b, c and d are stretches and translations of the graph, as in 2.3 Transformations, polynomials and modulus functions.

Real-world phenomena that repeat in regular cycles—ocean tides, hours of daylight, alternating current, or the height of a rider on a Ferris wheel—are modelled by transformed sine or cosine functions:

f(t) = a sin(b(t − c)) + d    or    f(t) = a cos(b(t − c)) + d
|a|
amplitude = (y_{max} - y_{min}) / 2 (vertical distance from mean line to peak)
d
vertical shift (principal axis y = d) = (y_{max} + y_{min}) / 2
b
angular frequency: Period T = 2*pi / b (in radians) so b = 2*pi / T
c
horizontal phase shift: for +a cos(b(t - c)) + d, c is the time of a maximum; for +a sin(b(t - c)) + d, c is a rising crossing of y = d

The graph of y = tan x

The tangent graph is not a sinusoid. Because tan x = sin x / cos x, it has vertical asymptotes wherever cos x = 0, that is at x = pi/2 + k*pi (k \in \mathbb{Z}). It has no amplitude, passes through (0, 0) and (pi/4, 1), increases on every branch, and repeats with period pi, not 2*pi.

Period of a stretched tangent: y = tan(bx) has period pi / b, so its asymptotes are pi / b apart. When you solve tan(bx) = k, consecutive solutions are also pi / b apart: add multiples of pi to the angle bx, not multiples of 2*pi.

Solving a quadratic trigonometric equation via double-angle substitution

[Paper 1 — No GDC] Solve cos 2x + 3 sin x - 2 = 0 for 0 <= x <= 2pi. [6 marks]

  1. Because the linear term is +3 sin x, we choose the version of cos 2x that contains only sin²x.

    (1 − 2 sin2x) + 3 sin x − 2 = 0 ⇒ 2 sin2x − 3 sin x + 1 = 0 [M1 A1]

  2. Treat u = sin x so 2u² − 3u + 1 = (2u − 1)(u − 1) = 0.

    (2 sin x − 1)(sin x − 1) = 0 ⇒ sin x = 12   or   sin x = 1 [M1 A1]

  3. sin x = 1/2 is positive in Quadrants I and II (reference angle π/6); sin x = 1 occurs at the top of the unit circle.

    sin x = 12 ⇒ x = π6, 5π6;    sin x = 1 ⇒ x = π2 [A1 A1]

x = π/6, π/2, 5π/6

Never divide a trigonometric equation by sin x or cos x if it could equal zero! To solve sin 2x = cos x, write 2 sin x cos x - cos x = 0 and factor as cos x (2 sin x - 1) = 0. Dividing by cos x throws away the roots cos x = 0 (x = pi/2, 3pi/2)!

The depth of water in a harbour is modelled by D(t) = 4.5 cos((pi/6)t) + 9.5 metres, where t is the time in hours after midnight. What is the maximum depth of water in the harbour?

  1. Since the maximum value of cos((pi/6)t) is 1, the maximum value of a cos(bt) + d is |a| + d.
  2. Add 4.5 + 9.5.
Dmax = 4.5(1) + 9.5 = 14 m
AHL 3.9–3.11

Reciprocal ratios, compound-angle identities and inverse trig functions

At Higher Level, we introduce the three reciprocal trigonometric functions—secant, cosecant, and cotangent—along with compound-angle formulas for A +- B and the inverse trigonometric functions arcsin, arccos, arctan.

sec θ = 1cos θ,   cosec θ = 1sin θ,   cot θ = 1tan θ = cos θsin θ,   1 + tan2θ = sec2θ,   1 + cot2θ = cosec2θ
sin(A ± B)
sin A cos B +- cos A sin B  (signs match)
cos(A ± B)
cos A cos B -+ sin A sin B  (signs flip!)
tan(A ± B)
(tan A +- tan B) / (1 -+ tan A tan B), and tan 2theta = (2 tan theta) / (1 - tan^2 theta)

Principal domains and ranges of inverse trigonometric functions (AHL 3.9)

Because sin x, cos x, tan x are periodic (many-to-one), we must restrict their domains to a single monotonic branch before they can have an inverse function. Memorize this table—especially what happens when you plug a negative input into arcsin(-x) versus arccos(-x)!

Principal domains, ranges and negative-input symmetry of inverse trig functions
Function Domain (input x) Principal range (output angle y) Quadrants covered Negative input rule
y = arcsin x [-1, 1] [-pi/2, pi/2] Quadrants IV and I Odd: arcsin(-x) = -arcsin(x)
y = arccos x [-1, 1] [0, pi] Quadrants I and II arccos(-x) = pi - arccos(x)
y = arctan x x in R ((-infty, infty)) (-pi/2, pi/2) Quadrants IV and I (horizontal asymptotes y = +-pi/2) Odd: arctan(-x) = -arctan(x)

Each inverse graph is the reflection of the restricted branch in the line y = x. The graph of y = arcsin x rises from (-1, -pi/2) through the origin to (1, pi/2). The graph of y = arccos x falls from (-1, pi) through (0, pi/2) to (1, 0). The graph of y = arctan x rises through the origin, with horizontal asymptotes y = -pi/2 and y = pi/2.

Complementary identity: For every x in [-1, 1], arcsin x + arccos x = pi/2, and cos(arcsin x) = sin(arccos x) = sqrt(1 - x^2).

Proving an inverse tangent sum using the compound-angle formula

[Paper 1 — No GDC] Without using a calculator, show that arctan(1/2) + arctan(1/3) = pi/4. [4 marks]

  1. Let A = arctan(1/2) and B = arctan(1/3), so tan A = 1/2 and tan B = 1/3 with 0 < A, B < π/4.

    tan A = 12,    tan B = 13 [M1]

  2. Substitute tan A and tan B into tan(A + B) = (tan A + tan B) / (1 − tan A tan B).

    tan(A + B) = 1/2 + 1/31 − (1/2)(1/3) = 5/65/6 = 1 [M1 A1]

  3. Since tan θ = 1 has multiple solutions, we must bound A + B using the range of arctan.

    Since 0 < tan A, tan B < 1, we have 0 < A, B < π4 ⇒ 0 < A + B < π2 ⇒ A + B = π4 [R1] [AG]

arctan(1/2) + arctan(1/3) = π/4

Not required from memory in IB Math AA HL: Product-to-sum and sum-to-product (factor) identities such as sin A + sin B = 2 sin((A+B)/2) cos((A-B)/2), as well as the Weierstrass half-angle substitution t = tan(theta/2), are not in the AA HL syllabus or Formula Booklet. If a Paper 3 investigation uses one of them, the formula will be given in the question for you to prove using sin(A +- B).

Symmetry properties of the trigonometric graphs (AHL 3.11)

Reflect the point P(cos theta, sin theta) on the unit circle and you get the angles below. Each row is also a symmetry you can see on the graphs: y = sin x and y = tan x have rotational symmetry about the origin (they are odd functions), and y = cos x is symmetric in the y-axis (an even function).

Symmetry identities from the unit circle
Angle Unit-circle move Identities What it says about the graphs
-theta Reflect in the x-axis sin(-theta) = -sin theta, cos(-theta) = cos theta, tan(-theta) = -tan theta sin and tan are odd; cos is even.
pi - theta Reflect in the y-axis sin(pi - theta) = sin theta, cos(pi - theta) = -cos theta, tan(pi - theta) = -tan theta The sine curve is symmetric about the line x = pi/2.
pi + theta Rotate by a half-turn about the origin sin(pi + theta) = -sin theta, cos(pi + theta) = -cos theta, tan(pi + theta) = tan theta The tangent graph repeats every pi.
pi/2 - theta Reflect in the line y = x sin(pi/2 - theta) = cos theta, cos(pi/2 - theta) = sin theta The cosine graph is the sine graph translated pi/2 to the left: cos x = sin(x + pi/2).

These identities are not in the Formula Booklet. If you forget one, derive it in one line from the compound-angle formulas, for example sin(pi - theta) = sin pi cos theta - cos pi sin theta = sin theta, or sketch the graph and read off the symmetry.

[AHL · Paper 1] Evaluate the exact value of cos(arcsin(3/5)).

  1. Let theta = arcsin(3/5), which is a Quadrant I acute angle with opposite side 3 and hypotenuse 5.
  2. By Pythagoras, the adjacent side is sqrt(5^2 - 3^2) = 4, so cos theta = 4/5.
cos(arcsin(35)) = √(1 − (35)2) = 45 = 0.8
