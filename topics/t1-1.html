Sequences, series and financial models

Guiding questionHow do we model patterns that grow by a fixed step versus a fixed percentage, and when does an infinite sum reach a finite limit?

What you need to be able to do

Tick each skill when you can do it confidently without looking at your notes. Everything on this page is SL core, examined on both SL and HL papers.

  • Perform operations with numbers in standard form a × 10^k where 1 <= a < 10 and k is an integer.
  • Find the n-th term u_n and sum of the first n terms S_n of an arithmetic sequence.
  • Evaluate and interpret sigma notation Σ_{r=a}^b u_r, counting b - a + 1 terms accurately.
  • Find the n-th term u_n and finite sum S_n of a geometric sequence, and solve threshold inequalities S_n > K.
  • Test for convergence (|r| < 1) and calculate the sum to infinity S_infty = u_1 / (1 - r).
  • Calculate compound interest, annual depreciation and the real (inflation-adjusted) value of an investment using the formula booklet and GDC Finance Solver.

Question decoder: spotting which technique to use

Sequence and financial problems often hide the mathematical formula behind everyday wording. Use this table to translate exam phrases directly into equations.

“Increases by a fixed amount each year / month” Arithmetic sequence with constant difference d = u_2 - u_1. Substitute u_1 and d into u_n = u_1 + (n - 1)d.
“Increases by p% each year” / “Loses p% of its value each year” Geometric sequence with r = 1 + p/100 (growth) or r = 1 - p/100 (decay). Watch the timeline! Value after t full years is PV × r^t (which is u_{t+1} if u_1 is at t = 0).
“Find the value in the n-th year” vs “Find the total over the first n years” Single year/step -> term u_n.

“a, b, c are consecutive terms of an arithmetic / geometric sequence” Arithmetic: b - a = c - b <=> 2b = a + c.
Geometric: b / a = c / b <=> b^2 = ac.		 Solve the resulting quadratic and check whether any root violates “distinct terms” or “convergent series”.
“Given that S_n = 3n^2 + 5n, find u_n” Recover terms from cumulative sums using u_1 = S_1 and u_n = S_n - S_{n-1} for n >= 2. P1 shortcut: Find u_1 = S_1 and u_2 = S_2 - S_1; if S_n is a quadratic with zero constant term, d = u_2 - u_1.
“The infinite series converges” / “Find the sum to infinity” Geometric series with |r| < 1 (-1 < r < 1) and S_infty = u_1 / (1 - r). Use |r| < 1 to reject extraneous roots of r, then substitute into S_infty.
“Find the least number of terms for which the sum exceeds K” Inequality S_n > K where n is a positive integer. P1: Isolate r^n and take logs (flip inequality if 0 < r < 1 since ln r < 0).

SL 1.1

Numbers in standard form

In scientific and mathematical work, very large and very small numbers are written in standard form (scientific notation):

a × 10k where 1 ≤ a < 10 and k ∈ ℤ
a
coefficient (mantissa), at least 1 and strictly less than 10 (for positive numbers)
k
integer exponent indicating the power of 10

On Paper 1 (no calculator), handle the coefficients a and powers of 10 separately:

  • Multiplication and division: Multiply or divide the coefficients, add or subtract the exponents (10^m × 10^n = 10^{m+n}, 10^m / 10^n = 10^{m-n}), and adjust if the new coefficient falls outside [1, 10).
  • Addition and subtraction: Rewrite both numbers with the same power of 10 first, add or subtract the coefficients, then normalize back to 1 <= a < 10.

Never leave a final answer as 14.2 × 10^5 or 0.65 × 10^{-3} when a question asks for the form a × 10^k. You will lose the final accuracy mark unless 1 <= a < 10 (1.42 × 10^6 and 6.5 × 10^{-4}). Also never write calculator shorthand like 6.5E-4 on an exam paper.

SL 1.2

Arithmetic sequences, series and sigma notation

An arithmetic sequence changes by adding a fixed step d, called the common difference, from one term to the next. Jumping from the first term u_1 to the n-th term u_n takes (n - 1) steps of size d.

Section 1.2 of your Formula Booklet gives both the n-th term and the two equivalent sum formulas for an arithmetic sequence:

un = u1 + (n − 1)d
Sn = n2(2u1 + (n − 1)d) = n2(u1 + un)
u1
first term
d
common difference (d = u_2 - u_1 = u_{n} - u_{n-1})
un
n-th term
Sn
sum of the first n terms (u_1 + u_2 + ... + u_n)

Sigma notation Σ

A series is the sum of the terms of a sequence. We write series compactly using sigma notation:

∑r = ab f(r) = f(a) + f(a + 1) + … + f(b) (number of terms = b − a + 1)

Whenever f(r) is a linear expression in r (such as 4r - 3), the terms form an arithmetic series with common difference equal to the coefficient of r.

Two classic sigma-notation traps:
1. In Σ_{r=4}^{15} u_r, there are 15 - 4 + 1 = 12 terms, not 15 - 4 = 11.
2. The “first term” of that sum is evaluated at the lower limit r = 4, not at r = 1.

Finding an arithmetic series sum from two given terms

In an arithmetic sequence, the third term is u_3 = 22 and the eleventh term is u_{11} = -10. Find the value of k given that the sum of the first k terms is S_k = 0 (where k > 0). [5 marks]

  1. Jumping from term 3 to term 11 takes 8 steps of size d.

    u11 − u3 = 8d = −10 − 22 = −32 ⇒ d = −4 [M1 A1]

  2. Use u₃ = u₁ + 2d = 22 to find u₁.

    u1 = 22 − 2(−4) = 30 [A1]

  3. Substitute u₁ = 30 and d = −4 into the Formula Booklet arithmetic sum formula.

    Sk = k2(2(30) + (k − 1)(−4)) = 0 [M1]
    Since k > 0, 60 − 4k + 4 = 0 ⇒ 4k = 64 ⇒ k = 16 [A1]

k = 16

In an arithmetic sequence, the first term is u_1 = 45 and the sixth term is u_6 = 15. Find the positive integer k for which the sum of the first k terms is S_k = 0.

  1. Use u_6 - u_1 = 5d to find the common difference d = -6.
  2. Set S_k = (k/2)(2(45) + (k - 1)(-6)) = 0 and solve 90 - 6k + 6 = 0.
5d = 15 − 45 = −30 ⇒ d = −6
2(45) + (k − 1)(−6) = 0 ⇒ 96 − 6k = 0 ⇒ k = 16
SL 1.3 & 1.8

Geometric sequences, finite series and sum to infinity

In a geometric sequence, each term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a non-zero constant r, called the common ratio. When r < 0, the terms alternate in sign (+ - + - ...).

Before you start: to solve for n in a geometric or financial question on Paper 1, you take logarithms of both sides. You need the laws of logarithms from 1.2 Exponents, logarithms and the binomial theorem.

Sections 1.3 and 1.8 of your Formula Booklet provide the n-th term, finite sum, and infinite sum formulas:

un = u1 rn − 1
Sn = u1(rn − 1)r − 1 = u1(1 − rn)1 − r, r ≠ 1
S∞ = u11 − r, |r| < 1
u1
first term
r
common ratio (r = u_2 / u_1)
Sn
sum of the first n terms
S∞
sum to infinity (exists if and only if -1 < r < 1)

Why does |r| < 1 give a finite sum to infinity? In the finite sum formula S_n = u_1(1 - r^n)/(1 - r), if -1 < r < 1, then raising r to larger and larger powers shrinks r^n -> 0 as n -> infty. The factor (1 - r^n) approaches 1, leaving S_infty = u_1 / (1 - r).

Finding the first term and common ratio from two non-consecutive terms

In a convergent geometric series with positive terms, the second term is 18 and the fourth term is 8. Find the sum to infinity S_infty. [5 marks]

  1. Translating words into equations with u₁ and r gives a system we can divide to eliminate u₁.

    u2 = u1r = 18 and u4 = u1r3 = 8 [M1]

  2. Dividing cancels u₁ and leaves r². Since all terms are positive, reject the negative root.

    r2 = 818 = 49 ⇒ r = 23 (since r > 0, |r| < 1) [A1 R1]

  3. Now that we have both u₁ and r, substitute into the Formula Booklet sum to infinity formula.

    u1 = 182/3 = 27 [A1]
    S∞ = 271 − 2/3 = 81 [A1]

81

A convergent geometric series has first term u_1 = 54 and third term u_3 = 24, with all terms positive. Calculate S_infty.

  1. Use u_3 / u_1 = r^2 to find the positive common ratio r.
  2. You should get r = 2/3. Now substitute u_1 = 54 and r = 2/3 into S_infty = u_1 / (1 - r).
r2 = 24 / 54 = 4 / 9 ⇒ r = 2 / 3
S∞ = 54 / (1 − 2/3) = 162

What is NOT needed for IB: You do not need the Ratio Test, Root Test, Integral Test, Comparison Test, or Alternating Series Test from university calculus. In IB Math AA HL Topic 1, infinite series convergence is tested solely via geometric series (|r| < 1) (and later via Maclaurin power series in AHL 5.19, on site page 5.4).

SL 1.4

Financial applications: compound interest and depreciation

When an investment earns compound interest at a nominal annual rate of r% compounded k times per year, each compounding period adds (r / k)% interest. Over n years there are k × n compounding periods, forming a geometric sequence.

Section 1.4 of your Formula Booklet gives the compound interest formula:

FV = PV × (1 + r / (100k))^{kn}
FV
future value after n years
PV
present value (initial principal at t = 0)
r%
nominal annual rate of interest
k
number of compounding periods per year (1 = annual, 2 = half-yearly, 4 = quarterly, 12 = monthly)
n
number of years

For annual depreciation (an asset losing r% of its value each year with k = 1), replace the plus sign with a minus sign: FV = PV × (1 - r / 100)^n. For inflation, prices grow by the factor (1 + i / 100)^n; to find the real value of an investment, use the interest rate minus the inflation rate, (r - i)%, as the rate in the formula or the Finance Solver.

Showing working with the GDC Finance (TVM) Solver on Paper 2: Whenever you use your calculator’s Finance Solver (for compound interest, depreciation or real-value problems), write down the list of TVM inputs in your answer box (N = …, I% = …, PV = …, PMT = …, FV = …, P/Y = …, C/Y = …). If you make a rounding slip, listing those inputs secures the method mark [M1]! Remember that cash outflows (money you invest or pay) and cash inflows (money you receive back) must have opposite signs (PV < 0, FV > 0).

May 2028 syllabus note: Financial applications (SL 1.4) are examinable in May 2028; the 2029 course may change, but that does not affect your exam. In Math AA, SL 1.4 covers compound interest, annual depreciation and real value with inflation, solved with the formula or the Finance Solver. A regular-deposit problem can still appear, but only as an application of the sum of a geometric series (SL 1.3).

What is NOT needed for IB: Annuity and loan amortization formulas (and loan-repayment problems on the Finance Solver) are Math AI topics, not Math AA.
